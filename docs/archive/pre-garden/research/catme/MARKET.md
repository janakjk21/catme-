# Market, Customers, Search, and Competitors

## Executive read

The United States is the primary market hypothesis, with the UK retained as a comparison. The US has a large cat-owning audience and mobile-game economy, but neither is the CatMe market. APPA's 2025 report release says 49 million US households own a cat (37% of households, based on 2024 data); ESA's revised 2025 report puts US video-game consumer spending at $60.8bn and mobile-game spending at $26.7bn. These establish category scale and competition for attention, not CatMe demand or a serviceable market. CatMe must prove the narrower overlap: people in a reachable segment who want a personalized digital cat, enjoy repeated interactions, and accept its creation and business model.

The UK remains useful for comparison: PDSA/Cats Protection estimate 10.2–10.5m owned cats, and Ukie reports £2.07bn UK mobile-game spending in 2025. Do not compare these totals directly with US household counts or US spending without harmonized definitions. The US should lead customer, search, storefront, and unit-economics work; use UK evidence to identify differences rather than assume US preferences transfer.

The competitive set is split across different jobs. Neko Atsume sells low-pressure observation and collection; Cats & Soup sells soothing idle activity, collection and frequent catalogue updates; My Talking Angela sells a familiar character with activities and customization; Pocket Love makes home design and snapshots the creative payoff; My Cat offers virtual cat care, touch response, AR, and memories. PetLovesYou and SoulBridge demonstrate adjacent photo/memory promises. The gap is not “no cat apps exist.” A candidate gap to validate is a **recognizable individual cat with satisfying, legible behaviour in a small home designed around that cat**, with less emphasis on huge worlds or task-heavy progression.

## Customer hypotheses

| Segment | Situation / job hypothesis | Possible CatMe value | Key reason it may fail | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current cat owner | Wants to play with or enjoy their own cat when away, busy, or between real interactions. | Identity, humour, short shared ritual, photo moments. | Digital copy seems inferior, uncanny, or redundant beside the real cat. | Best initial interview segment; unvalidated. |
| Cozy mobile-game player | Wants a calming short session and pleasant space. | Ambient cat behaviour, room decoration, discovery. | Existing cozy games offer more content and polish; personal-cat upload may be friction. | Plausible secondary segment. |
| Person unable to keep a cat | Wants cat-related play/companionship without real pet obligations. | A gentle digital cat with controllable care. | Expectations could drift into simulated care or therapeutic claims; many established alternatives. | Investigate; do not assume substitute equivalence. |
| Person with older pet photos / remembrance need | Wants a meaningful way to revisit their cat. | Opt-in memories and personal imagery. | A moving simulated pet could be unwelcome or distressing; existing memorial tools set different expectations. | Sensitive research only; not primary positioning yet. |
| Family / young player | Wants safe, simple, playful pet interaction. | Clear controls and funny cat moments. | Child safety, ads/IAP, permission and data handling; current prototype not polished for child use. | Defer as defined target until tested. |
| Creator / gift buyer | Wants a remarkable image/video or a personalized gift. | Shareable cat outputs or a gift code. | One-time content tool may not need an ongoing game; production/support burden. | Possible acquisition or one-off offer, not validated. |

APPA's 2025 US dog-and-cat report release also describes generational differences among cat owners and reports social platforms as a pet-product discovery influence for Gen Z. These are useful recruitment and channel hypotheses, not proof that social content will acquire CatMe users. Cats Protection's UK 2025 report says 74% of owners report their cat brings joy and 92% consider the cat part of the family; it also reports 1.5m households would like a cat and over half a million cannot due to landlords. Treat these as UK-specific emotional/access context, not US demand or app willingness to pay. [APPA report release](https://americanpetproducts.org/news/the-american-pet-products-association-appa-releases-2025-dog-cat-report), [Cats Protection report](https://www.cats.org.uk/about-cp/cats-report?token=521F21EC3A)

ESA's US mobile-game aggregate says 82% of Americans age 8+ play video games on mobile. This supports mobile as a plausible channel, while saying nothing about this concept's discoverability or retention. Sensor Tower's US iOS cat-game Q3 2025 analysis estimates materially different performance across The Battle Cats (action/strategy), My Cat (virtual pet), and Cats & Soup (cozy idle); its estimates are third-party, platform-specific, time-bounded signals rather than audited sales. The spread suggests “cat game” is not one customer job or genre. [ESA 2025 report](https://www.theesa.com/resources/essential-facts-about-the-us-video-game-industry/), [Sensor Tower cat-game analysis](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-5-cat-games-ios-us-q3-2025)

The question should not be “Are cat owners emotional about cats?” The better question is: **Which moment in a real cat relationship would a digital companion add to, and what evidence would make that addition feel worthwhile rather than artificial?**

## Market sizing discipline

Use a staged US-first model, not pet ownership multiplied by an invented percentage:

`reachable audience → qualified interest → store-page conversion → successful setup → retained players → payer conversion → net spend`

Do not yet assign conversion rates. Gather actual app funnel observations from a small test and use platform/category benchmarks only if the methodology and comparison set are disclosed. Keep separate:

- Total category context: 49m US cat-owning households (APPA 2025 report, 2024 data); 10.2–10.5m UK owned cats (different unit/source).
- Addressable audience: people in those geographies who use compatible smartphones and want a personalized companion / cozy cat game.
- Obtainable audience: people CatMe can reach through store search, social video, referrals, creator partnerships, or paid acquisition at sustainable cost.
- Paying audience: retained users who perceive value beyond the free experience.

ESA reports US mobile-game spending of $26.7bn in 2025, within a $60.8bn total US video-game market. Ukie reports £2.07bn UK mobile-game consumer spending in 2025, up 7.9%. Both aggregates include all mobile games; they are category context, not TAM or evidence that CatMe can acquire users profitably. [ESA](https://www.theesa.com/resources/essential-facts-about-the-us-video-game-industry/), [Ukie](https://ukie.org.uk/news/uk-video-games-market-reaches-8-76-billion-growing-7-4-in-2025)

## Search-intent map (not a volume ranking)

| Intent cluster | Example phrases to measure | Likely search expectation | CatMe risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Broad category | cat game, cat games, kitten game, cat simulator | Immediate play, often free and familiar genre. | Highly competitive, vague intent and potentially wrong game style. |
| Virtual pet | virtual cat, virtual pet cat, pet simulator, talking cat | Care, touch response, needs, customization. | User may expect talking, bathing, wardrobe and minigames. |
| Cozy / relaxation | cozy cat game, relaxing cat game, cozy pet game | Calm visual tone, easy sessions, no harsh pressure. | Requires consistent visual and sound quality; “cozy” is generic. |
| Identity / photo | make my cat a game, cat from photo, 3D cat from photo, turn my cat into a character | Fast recognizable image or model from user photo. | Searcher may expect a one-off creator, not an ongoing game. Phrases are hypotheses until measured. |
| Cat companionship | cat companion app, digital cat, my cat app | Presence, response, relationship or widget-like access. | “Companion” can imply conversational or wellbeing capability we do not offer. |
| Decoration | cat room game, decorate cat room, cat house game | Furniture catalogue, placement, multiple themes. | Requires a broad item catalogue and clear room editing. |
| Memory / loss | cat memorial app, pet remembrance, animate pet photo | Private, respectful remembrance and/or images. | High trust and emotional sensitivity; avoid playful game language unless users endorse it. |

Priority to measure first in the US: (1) virtual pet/cat game queries, (2) cozy/relaxing cat queries, (3) personalized cat/photo queries, (4) decorate/home queries, (5) remembrance queries as a separate lane. Search-volume evidence is currently absent. Record US English query wording and storefront autocomplete separately for Apple and Google; only then repeat shortlisted terms in the UK. Google Trends normalizes values to relative 0–100 interest; it is not absolute volume. Use Keyword Planner for ranges and record its settings; use store suggestions and competitor pages for language discovery. [Google Trends explanation](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533?hl=en-US), [Keyword Planner](https://ads.google.com/home/tools/keyword-planner/)

### US / UK language and store discovery hypotheses

These are seed phrases, not observed autocomplete or measured queries. US tests should include “cozy cat game,” “virtual pet cat,” “cat simulator,” “my cat game,” “make my cat 3D,” “cat from photo,” “cat room game,” and “relaxing cat game.” UK tests should include equivalent wording and spelling such as “cosy cat game,” while measuring whether “cat game,” “virtual pet,” or photo-personalization language changes expectation. Keep app-store search separate from web search: someone searching “cat from photo” may want a one-time image tool, whereas “virtual cat game” implies repeat play. No Keyword Planner export or comparable US/UK Trends series is in this evidence pack; volume is unknown.

Apple says store search uses text relevance and customer behaviour and surfaces title, subtitle, keywords, category, screenshots and other product-page elements. This makes an accurate promise and strong creative part of product research, not just launch marketing. [Apple guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/)

### Competitor evidence cards: what direct listing and review inspection can tell us

**Oliver the Virtual Cat:** the Google Play listing advertises a very broad virtual-pet bundle—care, 50 mini-games, multiplayer, room decoration, garden, stickers/cards, music, evolution and building blocks—and shows 10M+ downloads. It is evidence of a feature-rich mainstream positioning, not of a small-team recipe. The surfaced review sample includes both a positive response to varied mini-games/costumes and a report that seasonal purchases/progress disappeared after closing, plus a complaint about lack of tutorial/update. For CatMe this yields concrete checks: a smaller mechanic needs an immediately understandable first action; progress/items must be visibly saved; and breadth can mask a weak core loop. The complaints are individual, self-selected, and dated, so do not infer prevalence. [US Play listing/reviews](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.frojo.cat)

**Cat Choices: Virtual Pet 3D:** its store promise is choice-driven cat life, care and outcomes; it has ads and IAP and showed 10M+ downloads at access. Prominently surfaced reviews describe ad interruptions covering choices or dominating short play, despite describing the underlying choice loop as potentially fun. This is a vivid trust failure case, not proof that ads always harm retention. CatMe should keep any monetization out of the main input/action area, show the complete experience before presenting an offer, and avoid interruptions during an emotionally timed cat response. [US Play listing/reviews](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.cat.life.sim)

**AR Cat:** the listing promises placing cats into the user's own room using the camera, feeding, photos, collecting 21 cats, toys and laser play; Google Play showed 100K+ downloads. The presence of AR establishes a neighboring feature, but not that the camera makes the relationship more emotionally compelling. AR introduces permission, lighting/surface detection, tracking, camera privacy and a different visual match problem. Treat it as a later comparative test only if customers ask to see their personal cat in their actual surroundings; do not add it for novelty. [US Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.lily.ar.cat)

This is still a desk teardown, not a hands-on install audit of all 15 apps. Public listings and current screenshots can support competitor promise, offer and visual positioning; they cannot verify actual onboarding, controls, session feel, ad timing or current runtime performance. A complete direct comparison should record those fields through hands-on app use on a fixed device and date, then pair them with a defined review sample. Store install counts and ratings are platform-reported signals and are not equivalent to active users or causal product evidence.

### What the current product evidence says about small, focused games

The US Neko Atsume 2 storefront articulates an unusually short loop: place snacks/toys, then wait and observe; its listing says the experience stays simple and documents a Cat's Club subscription alongside its free/IAP structure. A recent surfaced user review says a costume and meow make them smile and asks for more backgrounds. This shows an example of value in tiny expressive details and setting variety, but one review cannot establish prevalence. [Listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/neko-atsume-2/id6499131935?platform=ipad)

Usagi Shima is a useful adjacent small-scale design reference: its listing pairs hand-drawn/animated visuals and calm no-time-limit framing with decoration, visits, a photo album, and a mix of expansion/tip/currency purchases. Its developer describes a simple “decorate, then visitors arrive” premise and solo/weekend origins; the developer also notes platform ecosystem integration was difficult. This is a closer scope analogy than a top-grossing live-ops title, but it is a rabbit island game with years of work and is not evidence CatMe can reproduce its audience. [Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/usagi-shima-cute-bunny-game/id1632728038), [developer account](https://godotengine.org/article/godot-showcase-usagi-shima/)

**Implication for CatMe:** the game does not need a giant audience or hundreds of items to be worth pursuing. It does need a cohesive, repeatable emotional moment and a transparent offer that pays for the real work. The owner-set near-term goal (10–15 people/day or roughly 100/month) is a product planning target, not evidence of current acquisition. Measure monthly active players, voluntary return, and direct qualitative satisfaction separately from downloads.

### Focused current US listing/review observations relevant to feel

A purposive read of the US Cats & Soup store page found several player statements that are unusually useful for CatMe: one reviewer praises smooth animation, consistent cute art, music collection, outfits/events and describes returning more than expected; another appreciates that ads are optional; a different reviewer says unfamiliar event currencies/tutorial gaps caused missed or wasted rewards; a decor-focused review complains furniture orientation limits their composition. This small, self-selected set is not representative and the reviews are from different dates (2022–2024), but it produces concrete design tests: cohesive style plus motion and sound can support the cozy feeling; optional monetization may preserve goodwill; more systems increase explanation burden; and room customization creates expectations about placement freedom. CatMe should emphasize a few composed objects and clear interactions rather than promise a free-build decorator unless that placement control works. [US Cats & Soup listing/reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/cats-soup-relaxing-cozy-games/id1581431235)

Neko Atsume's recent US review is another single user signal: the player reports that a special costume and meow make them smile, and asks for additional backgrounds; they dislike or feel uneasy about visiting/shared yard features. This suggests expression can come from a small detail and that social features may reduce privacy comfort for some players. It supports testing sound/costume/backdrop as optional expression—not assuming social sharing between players is required. [US Neko Atsume listing/reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/neko-atsume-2/id6499131935?platform=ipad)

Store-listed art signals (not independent visual ratings): Cats & Soup explicitly calls its look “fairytale-like illustration” and “ASMR”; Usagi Shima calls itself hand-drawn/traditionally animated and shows a decorative island; My Talking Angela 2 positions a highly animated 3D character through fashion/activity updates. These represent three distinct visual-product contracts. A CatMe screenshot should communicate its own distinctive cat and the exact interaction, not just “cute 3D cat in pretty room.” The source pages contain current screenshots, but CatMe still needs a controlled direct viewing task to determine which treatment users prefer.

### Review failure modes worth testing (not prevalence findings)

A purposive sample of publicly surfaced My Cat reviews from UK, US and Malaysia listings includes complaints about subscription screens obscuring entry, frequent ads, boredom while the cat sleeps, mismatch between advertising and the required paid feature, and confusion about whether a subscription is required. Some examples are from 2020–2025 and the selected comments are not a random sample. These do not prove such complaints are common across the market. They create concrete usability and trust tests for CatMe: can a first-time player find a free path; does the first session deliver the advertised experience; does the cat remain interesting during pauses; and do paid options clearly state what they unlock? [Review pages](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/my-cat-virtual-pet-games/id1480397119?platform=iphone&see-all=reviews) ([US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/my-cat-virtual-pet-games/id1480397119?see-all=reviews)) ([Malaysia](https://apps.apple.com/my/app/my-cat-virtual-pet-games/id1480397119?see-all=reviews)).

## Competitive whitespace hypothesis

Potentially useful distinction: **one named cat, one carefully composed home, small behaviours tied to real cat identity, and a relaxed visit with no failure for absence.** This could be clearer and more buildable than a huge cat-collection game or a generic life sim. The risk is that it becomes a beautiful but shallow screensaver after the photo reveal. Research must prove repeated player agency and variation.

The initial research should test a head-to-head concept board with three products: (A) personalized cat companion, (B) playful toy/room interaction game, (C) photo memory creator. Ask participants to choose, explain what they expect to do, and identify the product they would actually install. Do not lead with CatMe's existing technical achievement.
