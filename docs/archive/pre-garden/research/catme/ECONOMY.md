# CatMe Economy and Monetization Research

## What the market evidence does and does not say

The US is the primary economics market to investigate. ESA reports $26.7bn in US mobile-game spending for 2025, but this does not estimate CatMe's reachable audience, customer acquisition cost, or likely payer conversion. APPA's US pet-industry spending is not an app TAM. UK figures remain a comparison, not the basis of the first forecast. Competitors show several established models: free with ads and currency purchases (Neko Atsume 2), free with substantial event/cosmetic/catalogue IAP (Cats & Soup, Pocket Love), and free with subscriptions and virtual currency (My Cat / My Talking titles). These are evidence of competitor offers, not evidence that CatMe’s audience wants those offers.

Neko Atsume 2’s US listing shows individual in-game items and currency bundles in USD (observed tiers include $0.99–$3.99). This is a visible reference point for small virtual-goods prices in that product, not a price recommendation for a personalized model. Recheck the US storefront before any price test. [US listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/neko-atsume-2/id6499131935?platform=ipad)

## What comparable games actually sell, and what that suggests

Current US storefront examples show several distinct value exchanges:

These store pages prove which offers are currently presented, not why players buy them or how much revenue each item generates. The Neko Atsume community also contains conflicting preferences: one recent US review likes special-character costumes and meow sounds but asks for more purchasable backgrounds; this is a single customer statement, not a demand estimate. [Review surfaced on listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/neko-atsume-2/id6499131935?platform=ipad)

**Economics interpretation for CatMe's stated small goal:** at 10–15 people/day or ~100 people/month, avoid a business model that requires thousands of catalog items or daily live operations. A coherent, complete paid experience (or one-time unlock) is viable to test if users can understand the promise before paying. A free starter interaction with a single transparent purchase for an identity or behavior expansion is also plausible. The choice depends on first-use conversion friction and how much content the player needs to judge quality; competitor categories alone cannot settle it.

If “100 people a month” means 100 new installs, an illustrative 5% purchase rate at $4.99 produces 5 purchases / $24.95 gross in that month; at 10%, 10 purchases / $49.90 gross. These are arithmetic examples only, before store fees, taxes, refunds, generation, support or content time. If it means 100 active users each month, cohort retention and repeat purchases matter differently. At this scale, generation retries, support, privacy handling, and founder content time can dominate store commission. The first spreadsheet should model founder time as a cost even if it is not initially paid as salary.

### Which CatMe purchases have a defensible customer value?

| Purchase candidate | Player's reason to buy | Recommendation / evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Personal cat creation | Recognition, identity, “this is my cat.” | Best potential signature purchase, but high cost/quality risk. Let player experience starter play, then see a faithful sample/preview, price and failure/refund terms before committing. Test owner recognition first. |
| Interactive toy or furniture | New cat behavior plus visible object. | Stronger than a skin if it creates a distinct moment (box investigation, window watching, blanket kneading). Sell as a small behavior pack only after playable demonstration; animation/code QA cost must be included. |
| Room theme / extra room | Expression and a place to arrange/share. | Plausible one-time expansion; a new room can be relatively tangible. Do not sell dozens of decor pieces before confirming players enjoy arranging rooms. |
| Cosmetic skin/accessory | Self-expression and photos. | Works only if players want to style their real cat; preserve identity and avoid obscuring markings. Keep strictly optional; test with preview on the user's cat. |
| Extra personal cat | Multi-cat household representation. | Test switching and whether each cat needs distinct behaviors/room. Don't assume more cats improves retention. Could multiply creation costs. |
| Memories/photo export | Keepsake and sharing. | Consider a one-time creator/export add-on only if users ask. Local save, user control, deletion, and no cloud requirement may be more trust-aligned. Don't monetize grief pressure. |
| Subscription | Recurring updates, storage, multiple spaces, ongoing services. | Not recommended for first release. It requires continuing value and creates churn/support expectations. Reconsider only if users voluntarily request regular new content or secure cloud features. |
| Ads | Ad-supported access or optional reward. | Avoid forced/interstitial ads in an intimate 1:1 moment. For a tiny, calm game, optional tip or one-time ad-free support may preserve tone better; test, don't assume. |

### Price and offer experiments (not price recommendations)

Use a small randomized choice study after a participant has played the same polished demo. Show truthful offer cards, for example: (A) full starter experience free, optional $2.99 behavior/room pack; (B) free demo, $4.99 one-time full unlock; (C) free demo, $4.99 personal-cat creation after preview, with room/interaction included. Include “I would buy none” and ask what content is missing. These price points are anchored only to observed US competitor offers (Usagi Shima $4.99 expansion/tip and small store SKUs, Neko/Amy varied low-price goods); CatMe's costs and willingness-to-pay are unknown. Replicate with GBP in the UK using current UK storefront tiers; don't simply FX-convert US prices.

At this tiny scale, prioritize purchase clarity and trust over sophisticated virtual currency. Soft currency adds UI, balancing, earn/spend logic, and the perception that users must grind. Only add it if players enjoy collecting and the game has enough recurring content to support it. Avoid loot boxes, scarcity pressure and paid random outcomes; they add complexity and weaken the personal, low-pressure promise.

## Small-scale operating economics and acquisition

Treat 100 monthly people as a community/product-learning target, not a paid-media optimization target. Define new install, monthly active person, and payer separately. At 100 new installs, even a promising store-page experiment may have too few impressions to distinguish small lifts; do not spend to force statistical significance before the product promise is clear. Start with direct feedback loops, a few trusted creator/referral paths, and a simple source log. Compare cost and quality by source only when counts are visible. The best early channel is the one that brings people who understand the promise and return, not the one with the most clicks.

Run a simple monthly burden model alongside revenue:

| Cost/obligation | Small-scale question |
|---|---|
| Maker time | How many hours each month for fixes, new moments, support and store updates? Include unpaid time. |
| Cat generation | What is cost per accepted cat including failed attempts, retries, moderation and refunds? |
| Storage/transfer | How many MB per generated cat, how long kept, and what is cache versus customer content? |
| Customer support | How often do likeness, upload, restore, payment or deletion issues need a human response? |
| Content cadence | Can one new behavior/room addition be delivered without lowering device QA or animation quality? |
| Trust/privacy | What effort is required to explain and delete photos/models, respond to requests, and manage processor terms? |
| Store and tax | Which country-specific fee, tax and refund rules apply at actual release? Recheck current platform rules. |

Keep a complete core visit enjoyable without a purchase and without daily maintenance. A one-time purchase or modest paid unlock can fit better than a subscription if CatMe's value is a polished, self-contained moment; an ongoing charge needs continuing storage, content, or service that users actively value. This is a business-model hypothesis, not a finding. Add one-time room/behavior expansions only if they are both player-visible and cheap enough to support. A free starter plus personal-cat creation fee may better align variable generation cost, but only if users trust the preview and accept the price after trying play.

## Candidate things customers might value

| Offer hypothesis | Possible emotional/player value | Cost / fairness issue | Test before building |
|---|---|---|---|
| First personal cat | “That is my cat,” ownership and recognition. | Generation, retries, support, model storage and quality variance. High trust risk if paid before quality preview. | Show two honest sample results and a clear price choice after free first-room demo; measure selection and refund expectations. |
| Additional cat | Household expression, more than one real pet, collection. | More models; single active cat may limit use. | Ask multi-cat owners how they want to switch and whether each needs a separate room/identity. |
| Authored Studio Cat | Distinct look/personality without photo setup; reliable quality. | Requires art/animation/catalogue content. | Let participants choose between personal cat and authored cat for a concrete task; test purchase choice, not just liking. |
| Interactive toys | New behaviour, comedy, play variation. | Each toy can require code, animation, sound, tuning and QA. | Prototype one toy with 2–3 outcomes; compare value to a static toy skin. |
| Interactive furniture | Cat uses a window perch, box, bed, scratcher or blanket; changes room routine. | Navigation clearance and animation/interaction coverage. | Preview visible behaviours and ask what participants expect it to do. Test use before price. |
| Room themes / décor | Self-expression, aspirational home, seasonal atmosphere. | Content treadmill; visual assets can become clutter. | Room mood board and item placement test; find whether players want actual-home or fantasy-home styling. |
| Wearables/accessories | Personal expression and shareable images. | May obscure likeness; model/rig compatibility and animation clipping. | Test a few restrained accessories on different coat/shape cases. Defer full wardrobe. |
| Memory photos / album | Keep a moment, show family, preserve personal context. | Private data handling; export/deletion and sensitive use cases. | Test local-only photo capture and sharing controls; interview users separately about memory needs. |
| Gift experience | Let someone create or gift a cat/home. | Redemption support, privacy and photo consent; one-time acquisition cost. | Concept test gift flow with purchasers and recipients. |
| Subscription | Ongoing content, storage or premium convenience. | Needs recurrent delivered value; churn and billing trust. | Defer until retained users identify recurring value. Do not use it merely to cover uncertain generation costs. |
| Ads / rewarded ads | Can subsidize free access or optional rewards. | Interrupts intimacy and calm; child-sensitive context; review risk. | Test only optional placement after users establish value; measure interruption and trust. Avoid forcing an ad to pet/feed. |

The item-store design should show what an item looks like in the room and what it enables. A furniture purchase may have visual and behavioural value; those should be distinct in product copy. Do not claim a purchased toy creates an animation unless it does.

## Initial monetization hypotheses (not validated)

1. **Free base experience:** one complete cat/home interaction slice with no paywall on basic care, petting, or a welcome. This gives a player evidence before asking for personal photos or money.
2. **Potential one-time personalization purchase or fee:** could cover variable generation cost, but only if customers can see a preview, know turnaround/failure terms, and judge likeness before the transaction. A paid creation may be a creator-tool economy rather than a game economy.
3. **Optional décor/toy bundles:** sell only after a free core loop has proven value and the items add either visible expression or a new behavior.
4. **Later authored cats/content packs:** possible if players want alternatives and CatMe can ship new quality consistently.
5. **Defer subscription and aggressive ads:** current evidence does not show recurring CatMe value, and interruptions may conflict with a quiet companion promise.

Do not monetize bond, basic comfort, care access, or a simulated cat’s distress. Treat memorial features separately; never place a sensitive remembrance behind pressure messaging.

## Unit economics model to populate with real data

For a cohort:

`Net contribution = (paid purchases × average gross price × (1 − effective store/payment fee)) − (generation + retry + variable hosting/storage + refunds/support + acquisition spend + content/support fixed-cost allocation)`

`Break-even paying customers = fixed costs / (average gross price × (1 − effective fee) − variable cost per payer)`

Do not populate the model with made-up conversion. Track these measured inputs:

| Input | Required source |
|---|---|
| Price by country, VAT/tax handling | Store/product pricing settings and accountant/platform documentation. |
| Store fee | Current enrollment and geography-specific Apple/Google terms. Google currently documents changes based on install cohort and market; do not apply a blanket 15%/30% rate. [Google current fee page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en); [Apple program terms](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/). |
| Photo generation cost | Actual provider invoice/API usage, failed jobs, retries and moderation. No paid generation was performed in this pass. |
| Accepted-cat rate | Blind user acceptance under defined likeness criteria, by photo type and provider. |
| Storage and download cost | Asset size, CDN/storage pricing, retention/deletion policy, and usage. |
| Conversion, retention, purchases, refunds | Instrumented cohort data after permission/consent and a real test. Not current. |
| Acquisition cost | Organic/referral measured separately from paid-channel spend and attribution. No campaign spend authorized. |
| Content operations | Animation, art, localization, QA, support and release costs. Estimate with actual work plans. |

### Sensitivity illustration (arithmetic only, not forecast)

At a hypothetical $4.99 gross one-time sale, proceeds before tax, refunds, generation, hosting and all other costs would be $4.24 at a 15% fee or $3.49 at a 30% fee. These are simple arithmetic illustrations, not a statement of the fee actually applying to CatMe: enrollment, market, install cohort, date and store rules matter. A $4.99 price is not a recommendation. The relevant question is whether the remaining proceeds cover accepted generation and service cost while US users consider the result worth that price. Build the first model in USD; add a separately researched UK price/tax/store scenario later.

For scale intuition only, assuming **100 new installs** and a $4.99 one-time purchase (before all costs and any applicable store fee):

| Purchase rate assumption | Purchases | Gross receipts |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1% | 1 | $4.99 |
| 5% | 5 | $24.95 |
| 10% | 10 | $49.90 |

At 100 monthly active users, the model must instead use their actual cohort retention and repeat purchase behavior; do not reuse the install conversion row as if it were LTV. At 10–15 new people a day, the same arithmetic can be expanded to monthly installs only after the metric is defined. This scenario is not a forecast and deliberately excludes recurring revenue assumptions.

Build low / middle / high scenarios only after actual price, fee, generation, retry, accepted-rate and test conversion data exist. Vary each key parameter; show break-even and downside if a generation is rejected or refunded. Treat the first 100–500 test users as a learning cohort, not a stable LTV estimate.

## Highest value questions for customer research

- What would you buy if the app were free to try: personal cat, room, toy, memory export, or nothing? Why?
- Which item would you miss if removed after a week?
- Would you pay for a cat creation before seeing its result, after seeing a preview, or only after playing with a starter cat?
- Does a decorative room item matter more if the cat uses it? What behaviour would justify the price?
- Do you prefer a one-time purchase with a fixed result, a bundle, or ongoing subscription, and what ongoing value would justify renewal?
- How would you react to ads in a quiet visit? Which placements would feel interruptive or unacceptable?
- Would a gift purchase be meaningful; who chooses/uploads the photo and controls deletion?

Use realistic price-choice experiments after participants understand the product. Stated willingness-to-pay answers tend to be weak evidence; observe trade-offs between actual concept bundles and free features. Do not make real charges until product, store policy and customer support are ready.
