# CatMe Research Index

Last updated: 2026-09-24

## Purpose

Decision research for the customer, product, experience, technology, brand, and economics of CatMe. The United States is the primary market hypothesis; the UK is a comparison market, with other regions considered where useful. This is an active desk-research first pass. Internet evidence does not validate customer desirability, likeness quality, retention, or willingness to pay; those require participant tests.

## First-pass working view

- Most coherent first customer hypothesis: a current cat owner who wants a playful, recognizable digital version of their own cat. This is a hypothesis, not proven demand.
- Strongest initial product hypothesis: a small personal “cat moment” game: one recognizable cat, a carefully staged room, and brief invitation/response/play moments with clear endings. Photo personalization may intensify identity but should not carry the whole repeat-use promise.
- Main product risk: the photo-to-3D promise creates a high likeness and animation bar. The current known-good cat has only a walk clip, and the live photo-generation flow and physical phone experience are not validated.
- Main economic risk: high cost and unpredictability per accepted personalized cat combined with no proven repeat purchase or retention loop.
- Preliminary direction to test: an authored, mobile-ready cat rig with controlled identity customization and expressive behaviour may outperform fully generated geometry on quality and reliability. Benchmark it; do not assume it.
- Do not use loss or grief as the primary marketing angle without research with that audience. Do not sell affection, bond, or relief from pet neglect.

## Current game-design recommendation

Test CatMe as a **small personal cat-moment game**, not a scale-first virtual-pet business: one recognizable cat in a carefully staged, warm but lightly stylized room; brief behavior moments with anticipation, response, consequence and a natural stopping point; the player invites but does not command every action. Start by polishing a toy catch/miss/recovery or box-inspection sequence. The feeling should come from contingent attention and familiar cat behavior, not constant affection, daily care pressure, or photorealism unsupported by animation. The room should make the cat and interactions easier to read. See `EXPERIENCE.md` and `RECOMMENDATION.md` for assumptions and falsifiers.

Owner steering recorded 2026-09-24: make first-person presence the lead experience—walk to pour food, call the cat toward the player, throw a ball, and turn to follow it. Treat the third-person room camera as a comparison mode. The updated prototype supports walking and dynamic call placement in the Editor; food, moving toy tracking, phone control feel, and customer preference still need validation. See `FIRST_PERSON_CAMERA_PROTOTYPE.md`.

For the owner's small-scale goal (roughly 10–15 people/day or 100/month), test either a complete paid experience or a free starter with one transparent one-time identity/behavior offer. Competitors demonstrate cosmetics, functional items, room expansions, tips, subscriptions and paid bundles, but do not show which is right for CatMe. Subscription, ad-heavy, virtual-currency and high-volume live-ops models are not justified yet. See `ECONOMY.md`.

## Highest-priority next actions

1. Complete the 15-app evidence capture. The desk scan remains a promise/listing/review scan, not a standardized hands-on teardown of every app. Capture US/UK screenshots, onboarding, first interaction, offer timing, recent positive/critical reviews, accessibility/privacy claims, and evidence date.
2. Use signed-in store/search accounts to capture exact US/UK autocomplete and Keyword Planner/Trends exports. Do not publish search volumes unless export settings, date, geography, and units are recorded.
3. Build comparative phone-size boards and one complete interaction storyboard from existing CatMe assets, then run the user tests after research-only restrictions are lifted. Test three motion/art directions and warm vs funny moments.
4. Verify photo deletion, generated model backup, save continuity, reduced-motion and assistive-technology behavior on physical iOS and Android devices. Source review cannot establish runtime accessibility or migration behavior.
5. Populate the economic model with actual provider invoices, failed/retry rate, storage, store fees/tax, support time and observed cohort counts before choosing a purchase model.
